Kandue for Graders · Last updated: 2026-09-16

Kandue for Graders shows a TA or instructor which of their students on Canvas LMS still need a grade, longest wait first. It reads the course over your own Canvas session and never writes to it. No backend of its own. Kandue for Students, the companion for the person handing the work in, has its own policy.

What it reads

Your Canvas courses where you are a TA or teacher, and for the one you pick: the sections, the students enrolled (name, login id, section), the TAs and teachers, the assignments, each submission's status, timestamps, how late it was handed in, score and who graded it, and, if you read the split from them, the course's group sets. That is what the gradebook already shows you, read through Canvas's /api/v1 on your signed-in session, so the extension never sees, stores or transmits a password or token. Canvas's submission records carry the submitted text as well; the panel keeps only the status, times, score and grader, and never stores or shows the work itself. It also asks Canvas for each submission's rubric, so that work a grader has begun but not yet scored can be told apart from work nobody has opened; of that, only the yes-or-no is kept, never the rubric's points or comments.

Where it stays

  • The course stays on your device. The students, submissions and grades last read for the course you picked are kept in chrome.storage.local, so the queue is on screen when you open the panel rather than after a wait. The next refresh replaces them, and they clear when you remove the extension.
  • Settings stay local. Your Canvas address, the course, who grades whom, and a grading sheet you drop in as a CSV live there too, and clear the same way.
  • Nothing is transmitted. The only servers contacted are your own school's Canvas and, if you connect one, Google Sheets. There is no Kandue server, no account, no analytics and no third party.

When it reads Canvas

When you open or refresh the panel; about every half hour, so the number on the toolbar icon is current; and after a page finishes loading on your Canvas, which is usually the moment grading has just changed something. A refresh is skipped when what it already has is recent enough. To tell whether a page that loaded is your Canvas, it compares that tab's address with the Canvas address in your settings. It reads nothing else from any page, and no tab outside the sites you have granted it.

A grading sheet

If your TA team keeps who-grades-whom in a Google Sheet, pasting its link asks your permission for docs.google.com at that moment, never at install. The sheet is then read over your own Google session, the way Canvas is, with no token and no change to its sharing. Leave the link out and the permission is never asked for.

Nothing is written

Every request is a read. The extension enters no grade, posts no comment and sends no message; grading stays in SpeedGrader. It holds no cookies permission and has no path to the token Canvas requires for a write.

Students' data

What the panel shows you is what your institution's Canvas already lets you see as a grader, on the device you are signed in on. The copy it keeps stays on that device. Your institution's rules for that access apply to the panel as they do to SpeedGrader.

Not done

No tracking, analytics, or ads. Nothing is sold or shared, because nothing is collected. The extension sets no cookies of its own.
